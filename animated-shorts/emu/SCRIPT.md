# "The War the Army Lost to Birds" — original script v1 (2026-11-02)

Facts (with sources):
- Nov-Dec 1932, Campion district, Western Australia. WWI veterans had been settled on wheat farms; ~20,000 emus
  moved into the farmland after breeding and ate and trampled the crops. (Wikipedia "Emu War"; Britannica "Emu War")
- The Defence Minister sent Major G.P.W. Meredith (Royal Australian Artillery), Sgt S. McMurray and Gunner
  J. O'Halloran with two Lewis guns and 10,000 rounds. (Wikipedia; Australian Geographic 2016)
- First days: the emus scattered out of range in small groups. At a dam ~1,000 emus approached an ambush; the gun
  jammed after about a dozen were killed. (Wikipedia; Britannica)
- A Lewis gun was mounted on a truck: the truck could not keep up and the ride was too rough to aim or fire.
  (Wikipedia; Mental Floss; We Are The Mighty)
- Emus run at up to ~50 km/h. Meredith reported each mob had its own leader, "a big black-plumed bird fully six feet
  high", keeping watch. (Wikipedia, quoting Meredith)
- Meredith claimed 986 kills with 9,860 rounds (10 rounds per bird) and said a division with "the bullet-carrying
  capacity of these birds" could face any army in the world. The troops were withdrawn in December 1932.
  (Wikipedia; Britannica; History Hit)

## Voice-over (~230 words)
In 1932, the Australian army went to war against emus, and lost.
After the First World War, thousands of old soldiers were given farms in Western Australia, to grow wheat.
Then twenty thousand emus showed up, and started eating it.
The farmers asked for help, so the army sent a major, two soldiers, two machine guns, and ten thousand bullets.
How hard could it be?
On day one, the emus simply ran off in every direction, at fifty kilometres an hour.
Next, the soldiers set an ambush at a watering hole. A thousand emus walked right in. The gun fired, hit about a dozen, then jammed.
So they bolted a machine gun onto a truck. But the emus outran the truck, and the bumpy fields made aiming impossible.
Wait, how do trained soldiers miss a bird the size of a person?
You see, emus don't move like one big herd. They split into small groups, and the major said each group had a tall lookout, watching for danger. Aim at one group, and the rest are already gone.
And when a bullet did hit, a big emu often just kept running.
The major claimed almost a thousand birds, at ten bullets each.
He said that with soldiers who could carry bullets like these birds, he could face any army in the world.
By December, the army went home.
The emus stayed.
