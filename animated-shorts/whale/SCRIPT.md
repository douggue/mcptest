# "The Whale That Came Back Down" — original script v1 (2026-11-02)

Facts (with sources):
- Nov 9 1970: a ~45 ft, ~8 ton dead sperm whale washed ashore near Florence, Oregon. The Oregon State Highway
  Division (responsible for beaches) decided on Nov 12 to blow it up with about half a ton of dynamite, expecting
  scavengers (seagulls) to eat the small pieces. (Mental Floss; KOMO/KVAL 2020; Offbeat Oregon; Wikipedia "Exploding whale")
- A KATU TV crew (reporter Paul Linnman) filmed it; spectators watched from the dunes. Blubber rained down on the
  crowd and nearby cars. (KATU 2020; Dave Barry, "The Exploding Whale")
- Walter Umenhofer, who had explosives experience, told the crew it was too much dynamite; a ~300 lb slab of blubber
  crushed the roof of his new Oldsmobile 88. The state paid for the car. (Jalopnik; Register-Guard 2008; Mental Floss)
- The blast scared away the seagulls; the crew buried the remains. (Wikipedia; Mental Floss)
- 2020: Florence opened Exploding Whale Memorial Park (named by public vote). (KOMO 2020)

## Voice-over (~225 words)
In 1970, the state of Oregon tried to get rid of a dead whale with half a ton of dynamite.
A forty-five-foot sperm whale washes up on a beach near the town of Florence. It weighs eight tons, and it really, really stinks.
In Oregon, the beaches count as highways, so the job goes to the highway department, the people who usually blast rocks.
Their plan: blow the whale into tiny pieces, and let the seagulls eat them.
A crowd gathers on the dunes, and a TV crew starts rolling.
One man in the crowd, who'd worked with explosives, warns them it's way too much dynamite. Nobody listens.
Three, two, one.
The whale goes up. And then, it comes back down.
Chunks of blubber rain onto the crowd, the cars, and the TV crew.
Wait, why didn't it turn into tiny pieces?
You see, an explosion pushes wherever it's easiest to go. Ever stepped on a ketchup packet? It doesn't disappear, it squirts out the weak side.
The sand under the whale pushed back, so the blast went up, throwing big slabs instead of crumbs.
And the bang scared away every seagull on the beach.
So the crew buried the leftovers anyway.
Oh, and that man who warned them? A slab of blubber crushed the roof of his brand new car.
Fifty years later, the town opened Exploding Whale Memorial Park.
